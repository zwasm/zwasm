# ADR-0208: vendor the official wasi-testsuite wasm32-wasip1 corpus and gate on it (D-582)

> **Doc-state**: ACTIVE

- **Status**: Accepted (2026-08-18 — maintainer sign-off on PR #183; the
  official suite is the bar). **D3 superseded by ADR-0225 (2026-09-02)**: the
  step blocks with a per-OS `known_table` instead of carrying
  `continue-on-error`, and runs on pull requests as well as the merge. D1 and
  D2 stand. The "Ratchet the 14 failures instead of running advisory" entry
  under `Alternatives rejected` is superseded with it — its ground, "Nothing in
  the repo ratchets failures today", was already false when written
  (`test/aot/aot_process_diff.zig` has carried that mechanism since
  `4ac56549a`, 2026-07-09).
- **Date**: 2026-08-14
- **Front**: B-hardening (D-582 infrastructure, D-583 the behaviour gaps it exposes)
- **Findings base**: measured conformance run 2026-08-14 at `2e40d4314` on
  x86_64-linux, with a wasmtime 47.0.3 control through the identical harness.

## Context

`zig build test-wasi-p1` walked `test/wasi/` — three hand-written fixtures:
`hello`, `env_echo`, `proc_exit_42`. The step is wired into `test-all`, so it
ran on all three OSes on every PR and read like preview1 coverage. The
official `WebAssembly/wasi-testsuite` `wasm32-wasip1` suite has **72** tests.
Nothing in the tree could catch a preview1 behaviour regression.

Running the official suite for the first time (official python runner, a
zwasm adapter, fresh preopen per test) gives **58/72 on `--engine interp`**
and **54/72 on `--engine jit`**. wasmtime 47.0.3, through the identical
harness in the same session, scores **72/72** — so the failures are zwasm
gaps, not harness artifacts.

This does **not** contradict §11.1 or ADR-0161. That row records the preview1
**syscall surface** at 46/46, up from an earlier 21/46-subset overclaim, and
the surface is complete. What no gate ever checked is whether those syscalls
**behave to spec**. Surface completeness and semantic correctness are
different claims, and only the first was ever measured.

Two measurement traps were found on the way, both of which shape the decision
below:

1. **A reused preopen tree hides failures.** `dangling_symlink` fails on a
   clean tree 20 times out of 20, leaves `dangling_symlink_symlink.cleanup`
   behind, and every later run against that same tree passes. The first pass
   of this measurement recorded it as flaky for exactly this reason.
2. **An unpinned engine measures the JIT.** The CLI default `.auto` prefers
   the JIT, so a lane that does not pin the engine reports JIT numbers under
   a preview1 heading. The lanes diverge by four AssemblyScript tests (all four are in that suite).

## Decision

### D1 — Vendor the upstream prebuilt binaries under `test/wasi/wasip1_official/`

`scripts/vendor_wasip1_official.sh` pulls the `prod/testsuite-base` prebuilt
`wasm32-wasip1` binaries, their per-test manifests, the per-language
`manifest.json` suite descriptors, and the `fs-tests.dir` preopen trees at a
pinned SHA; `wasm-tools strip` runs before commit. Same deliberate-bump-only
rule as `vendor_wasip3_official.sh` (edit `PIN_SHA`, re-run, commit), plus a
per-language expected-count assertion so an upstream corpus change fails
loudly instead of silently widening or narrowing what we claim to run.
Apache-2.0; `legal/THIRD_PARTY.md` carries the row.

Stripping is load-bearing, not hygiene: the raw corpus is 96.5 MiB, of which
about 94 MiB is Rust debug custom sections. Stripped it is 5.5 MiB, against
the 17 MiB of already-vendored wasip3 corpus.

The per-language split is preserved because upstream ships one suite
descriptor per language and the runner reports per-suite counts the way the
official python runner does. The preopen trees are reproduced verbatim: the C
suite ships `fopendir.dir/` and `writeable/`, and a flattened copy makes
`fdopendir-with-access` and `pwrite-with-access` fail — two tests that pass
against a faithful tree, measured both ways. (`pwrite-with-append` fails
either way; it is one of the 14 real gaps, not a vendoring artifact.)

**This amends ROADMAP §11.2.** Original row:

| WASI testsuite | similar — pinned external repo | regenerated |

New row: the prebuilt binaries are committed, pinned and regenerated by
script.

The row went stale in 2026-08, when ADR-0205 D3 committed the wasip3 corpus
without amending it. Note that D3's justification is **not** the one used
here: it vendored because local regeneration was *impossible* — the borrowed
wasip2 wasi-libc emits 0.2.6 imports until upstream ships a wasip3 wasi-libc,
so D-523's local-regen half was reframed external-blocked. That barrier does
not apply to preview1, which is buildable today.

The justification for preview1 is the other one §11.2 already states:
**heavyweight toolchain outputs**, the same clause under which realworld
`.wasm` samples are committed. Rebuilding these needs Rust, clang+lld and
AssemblyScript, which is why upstream ships them prebuilt on a dedicated
branch. Two different reasons, one resulting shape, and the row now covers
both because it describes what is committed rather than why.

### D2 — Expose it as its own test layer, not inside `test-all` yet

`zig build test-wasi-p1-official` runs two lanes, `interp` and `jit`,
separately.

The step is **not** added to §11.1's table. That table has not been touched
since the Phase-0 bootstrap (`9bd21b2fc`): it lists 8 steps where `build.zig`
declared 42 at the findings base (31 of them `test*`; the count only grows —
ADR-0209/0210 pushed it to 47), and `test-wasi-p1`, `test-wasi-p3`, `test-component-spec`,
`test-spec-simd` and `test-spec-assert` are all absent — including steps added
as recently as the ADR-0205 campaign. It is a frozen Phase-era overview, not a
live registry, and adding one step to it would misrepresent it as maintained.
This ADR's load-bearing §11 edit is the §11.2 data-policy row in D1.

The step deliberately stays **out of `test-all`** while D-583 is open.
`test-all` is the blocking gate and the corpus is 14 red today, so folding it
in now would red every PR and force a choice between reverting it and
freezing the 14 as an accepted state. Both alternatives, and why an advisory
job is preferred over a failure ratchet, are in `Alternatives rejected`.

The runner asserts the corpus is actually there before believing a green
result: a missing root exits 1, and so does a run whose executed-test count
is not the vendored 72. The second check is not redundant — suite
directories that exist but hold no `.wasm` otherwise sum to "0 passed, 0
failed" and exit 0, which is ADR-0174's own failure mode (a leg reporting OK
while every spec category was `pass=0`) reproduced one layer up. The first
draft of this runner had exactly that hole. Per-suite counts are printed
rather than an OK/NG verdict for the same reason.

The existing three-fixture `test-wasi-p1` step stays. It exercises the
`.expected_exit` / `.env` sidecar path and costs nothing; it is simply no
longer load-bearing as a conformance claim.

### D3 — Run it as an advisory step in `gate`, with a written exit condition

> **SUPERSEDED by ADR-0225 (2026-09-02).** Kept as written; what shipped is in
> ADR-0225 D1/D3.

The step runs at the end of the existing `gate` job on all three OSes with
step-level `continue-on-error: true`. GitHub's contract is that a failing
`continue-on-error` step keeps `outcome: failure` — so the red is visible in
the run — while its `conclusion` is `success`, which is what propagates to the
job and therefore to the `needs.gate.result` that `ci-required` reads. The
existing "Install wasmtime (reference oracle, best effort)" step in the same
job already relies on this.

**A step rather than a job of its own.** A separate job would repeat the Zig
install and rebuild zwasm from a cold `.zig-cache` on all three runners —
nothing caches `.zig-cache`, only `zig-pkg` and the Zig toolchain — so it
would pay a second full build per PR to run two lanes over 72 small binaries,
in a workflow whose own comments put cold-cache builds at 2.5–4.5 min each.
As a step it reuses what `ci_gate.sh` just built.

This is a **time-boxed** advisory in the ADR-0174 sense ("a deliberate,
user-sanctioned mode, NOT a silent skip"), not an indefinite workaround. The
exit condition is D-583's discharge: when the 14 engine-independent failures
AND the 4 JIT-only ones go green on all three OSes, `test-wasi-p1-official`
moves into `test-all` and this step is deleted. Both classes, because the move
wires both lanes and `test-all` blocks every PR. `no_workaround.md` forbids indefinite workarounds
specifically; naming the condition in the debt row and in the job comment is
what keeps this one bounded.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Fetch the corpus at CI time instead of committing it.** This is §11.2's
  literal current wording, and it is the option that most preserves the
  existing policy. Rejected because it puts a GitHub fetch on the critical
  path of every gate run on every OS — the exact failure the Zig-package
  cache commit removed after a transitive dep fetch flaked twice in one day
  ("unable to discover remote git server capabilities"). A conformance corpus
  is a worse thing to make network-dependent than a build dep, not a better
  one.

- **Build the corpus ourselves in the `.#gen` lane** rather than taking
  upstream's binaries, the way realworld fixtures are produced. Two reasons
  against, one practical and one substantive. Practical: `flake.nix`'s gen
  lane has rustc and clang+lld but **no AssemblyScript**, so 12 of the 72
  tests could not be built without extending it. Substantive, and the reason
  that would still hold if it were extended: upstream's binaries *are* the
  conformance artifact. A corpus we compiled ourselves would demonstrate that
  zwasm passes tests zwasm built, which is a materially weaker claim and one
  that no longer matches what "the official suite" means to anyone reading it.

- **Fix D-583's 14 failures first, then land corpus + runner + `test-all`
  wiring in one green PR.** Genuinely viable, and it would need neither the
  advisory step nor D3. Rejected on sequencing: the fix work spans several
  cycles, and during all of them there would be no gate — the preview1
  surface would stay exactly as unmeasured as it has been, and a regression
  landing mid-fix would be invisible. Measuring first also means the fix work
  gets a per-commit signal instead of a manual re-run. The cost is that the
  advisory step exists for a while, which D-583's discharge condition bounds.

- **Ratchet the 14 failures instead of running advisory.** The project does
  use one-way ratchets — `scripts/check_skip_impl_ratchet.sh` gates
  `skip_impl_history.yaml` and fails on any increase not accompanied by an
  ADR-citing exempt row — so this is house-shaped, not forbidden, and the
  earlier framing of it as a banned "baseline" was wrong. Rejected on what
  the existing ratchet actually measures: **skips**, i.e. directives the
  runner could not attempt. That is a coverage metric, and freezing it says
  "we have not got to these yet". A ratchet over **failures** would freeze a
  correctness metric and say "these wrong answers are the accepted state".
  Nothing in the repo ratchets failures today — `bench/results/` holds
  skip-impl, size and bench series only, and there is no xfail mechanism. An
  advisory step reports the same number without asserting it is acceptable.

- **Fold the step straight into `test-all`.** Reds every PR until D-583
  closes, which forces either a revert or one of the two options above.

- **Run one lane on `.auto` instead of two pinned lanes.** Halves the CI cost
  and measures the wrong thing: `.auto` prefers the JIT, so the number would
  be the JIT's under a preview1 heading. The divergence is 4 tests today and
  nothing bounds it — the two engines share the WASI host but not the path
  into it.

## Consequences

- `test-all` and the required check are unchanged; the merge gate does not
  move in this ADR.
- The 14 engine-independent failures (D-583) and the 4 JIT-only failures
  become tracked, reproducible, and visible on every PR across three OSes,
  where before they were unmeasured.
- `.dev/debt.yaml` gains D-582 (this infrastructure) and D-583 (the behaviour
  gaps). D-582 discharges when the step reports on all three OSes; D-583
  discharges the advisory flag.
- Single-host caveat: the 58/72 and 54/72 numbers are x86_64-linux. The
  three-OS job is what turns them into a cross-platform claim.
